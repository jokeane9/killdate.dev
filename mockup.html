Why I build in public

May 01 2026 · 4 min read · process

I've been building side projects in private for years. Quietly. No blog, no tweets, no updates. Just me, a terminal, and a graveyard of half-finished repos.

The problem isn't motivation — I have plenty of that. The problem is accountability. Without an audience, even a tiny one, it's too easy to let things slip into "I'll get to it eventually" territory.

What changed

Earlier this year I started shipping things under a domain name instead of just a GitHub handle. Something about having a killdate.dev URL made it feel real. Like it existed in the world, not just on my laptop.

Shipping is a skill. You get better at it by doing it repeatedly, not by waiting until something is perfect.

So I'm writing it down. Not for an audience — or at least, not primarily. For the same reason I keep a dev journal: because writing forces clarity, and clarity is the most underrated productivity tool in software.

What this blog will be

Notes on the process. What I'm building, why I made certain calls, what broke, what worked. Tooling I'm using. Infrastructure decisions. The kind of thing I wish I could have read when I was figuring this stuff out.

No guarantees on cadence. But I'll ship something when there's something worth shipping.
